      How it works

      From link to library in three steps.

      Paste a link

      Copy any Spotify song, album or playlist link and drop it in. Use Bulk mode for several at once.

      Choose your format

      Pick MP3, M4A, FLAC or WAV and a quality level. Preview a playlist and untick tracks you don't need.

      Download

      Watch each track finish, save them one by one, or grab everything in a single ZIP.

      Features

      Everything a music library needs. Nothing it doesn't.

      Songs, albums, playlists

      One tool for every kind of Spotify link, single or in bulk.

      Pick your tracks

      Preview a playlist and select only the songs you want to keep.

      Tagged & organised

      Title, artist, track number and cover art are saved in each file.

      Four formats

      MP3 and M4A up to 320 kbps, plus FLAC and WAV containers.

      Fast & parallel

      Three tracks download at the same time, with live progress for each.

      Cancel & retry

      Stop any time, and retry only the tracks that failed. No starting over.

      FAQ

      Good to know.

      Where does the audio come from?

      TuneFetch reads track details from Spotify, then finds the best matching audio on YouTube by title and duration. Quality depends on that source, so FLAC and WAV are lossless containers but cannot add detail that isn't there.

      Is there a playlist size limit?

      Spotify's public page lists up to about 100 tracks per playlist, so very large playlists may be shortened.

      Why does it ask for ffmpeg?

      ffmpeg converts the downloaded audio into your chosen format. Install it once with winget install Gyan.FFmpeg and restart the app.

      Where are my files stored?

      Files are created in the downloads folder next to the app, grouped by job, and removed automatically after 24 hours.

      Is TuneFetch open source?

      Yes. It is released under the MIT license. Read the code, fork it or contribute on GitHub.
